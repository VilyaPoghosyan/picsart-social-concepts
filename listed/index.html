Listed

Better listings from the photo you already have. made with Picsart

Channel concept one-pager · Instagram @listed.hq · TikTok @listedstudio (proposed) · status: concept approved, pipeline prototyped, pre-launch

If you read only this

What
A faceless page (no presenter on camera) for people who sell products online. Every post starts from a messy phone photo of a product, shows what Picsart turns it into, then shows exactly which Picsart tools did it and how.
Who
Small online sellers on Etsy, Depop, Amazon, TikTok Shop, Shopify. They need new product photos every week, and buyers judge them on photos first.
Why Picsart
Each post ends one tap from the Picsart tool it just showed. The page is built to move sellers into Picsart, not to collect views.
How
Fully automated. Software picks the product, Picsart's own AI tools make every "after", a renderer cuts the videos, a quality check runs, a person only approves. About 4 to 20 credits (Picsart's unit of payment for AI generation) and 2 to 3 human minutes per product.
Will it work
Main metric: median views per post against our own new-account baseline. Picsart metric: link taps per 1,000 views. Decision after 12 posts over 4 weeks: scale at 3× the baseline, kill under 1.5×.
Evidence
Strong on demand (before/after is a 105-billion-view format; buyers rank photos above price). Weak on precedent: nobody runs a faceless, daily, tool-exact seller page yet, so we are first.

The three post types, with the real examples we made

A pillar is a recurring type of post. One product gives a thread: a Before/After on day one, its Tutorial on day two, and a slide in the Sunday Carousel. Below: the ceramic mug thread exactly as the pipeline produced it. The "before" is an AI-made photo labelled as AI; every "after" is a real Picsart output.

1 · Before / After, 7 s. The share. Phone photo with the hook, a wipe to the Picsart result, end card. Caption: "Same mug. Same phone. One Picsart edit. Comment FIX and I'll send you the exact tool. made with Picsart".
2 · Tutorial, 18 s. The proof. Result first, then Step 1 "Open Picsart", then each tool with its icon and exact menu path: Remove Background, Change Background, Enhance. Every step shows that tool's real output.
3 · Carousel, Sunday, 7 slides. The save. Cover with the week's hook, one split slide per product (before left, after right), closing slide with the call to action. On Instagram carousels earn about nine times the saves of a single image.

The Tutorial also has a Flow variant: it replays Picsart Flow's canvas (photo node, prompt node, image model, video model, save) so a seller can build the chain once and run it on every new product. The ad clip for a product comes out of that Flow.

Why they come back. The need repeats: new inventory every week means new photos every week. Every post answers one of three questions, what can Picsart do for my photo, which tool and how, is this for my kind of product too, and the answer is always reproducible. Sellers search for the tool, not the craft: about 255,000 Google searches a month for "remove background" and "background remover" (Semrush snippet, 2026, estimate).

Competitors and the gap. Photoroom (about 800K TikTok, 1.9M Instagram, read 2 Oct 2026) is the only brand account built around product photos for sellers, and it is almost entirely background removal. Pixelcut has no owned TikTok. Shopify, Etsy and eBay post seller stories, not photo help. Creators who teach product-photo hacks are presenter-led and show tricks, not tools. Picsart has roughly eight times Photoroom's US active users (Sensor Tower, Q3 2025) and no voice in this niche. Nobody runs a faceless, daily page where every step is reproducible in one app.

Why this concept for Picsart

Background removal gets a seller in the door. Picsart's generative tools make the before/after dramatic, and no one else in the niche shows them. Every thread is tagged with one of five seller jobs, so the feed shows Picsart's range, not one trick. All five were produced by the pipeline during prototyping.

How it is made

Per product

2 posts + 1 carousel slide · about 4 to 20 credits · 2 to 3 human minutes · under 10 machine minutes.

Per week

7 posts on Instagram and 7 on TikTok, same assets: Mon/Wed/Fri Before/After, Tue/Thu/Sat Tutorial, Sun Carousel. About 40 to 60 credits, about 15 human minutes plus comment replies.

Rules on every post

AI label in frame and the platform's AI flag set · caption ends "made with Picsart" · exactly five hashtags (both platforms cap at five since 2025) · hook names the product, never says AI · Picsart colours, Gilroy, official end card.

Will it work, and how we'll know

Hypothesis. Small online sellers will share, save and follow a faceless page that shows a real phone photo turning into a listing-ready image and names the exact Picsart tool, and a meaningful share will tap through to that tool.

First experiment. Hook test on the Before/After: problem-first ("Messy background? Fixed in 3 taps") against result-first ("Same mug. Same phone. One edit"). Six posts per arm, metric full-watch rate, guardrail follows per 1,000 views, pre-registered. Second: "Comment FIX" against a plain bio link, measured in link taps.

Scale when, after at least 12 posts over 4 weeks, median views exceed 3× the baseline and followers are growing: move toward two posts a day.
Kill when, after 12 posts, median views are under 1.5× the baseline and no post reached 10× the page's own median: archive the kit, keep the account, replace from the backlog within a week.

No tuning before the 12-post read except fixing broken AI disclosures. Honest read: demand and cost are strong; the view floor is a guess until week two, and the Instagram comment-to-link automation is not set up yet.
