If you read only this

What
A faceless page (no presenter on camera) of tutorials. Every post opens on an edit people already see in their feed, then shows the exact Picsart tool, path and prompt to make it in minutes.
Who
People who make their own content: TikTok and Instagram creators, bloggers, small YouTubers. Under every viral AI edit they write the same comment: "how?"
Why Picsart
Each post ends one tap from the tool it used, with the prompt to paste, so the viewer's first success happens inside Picsart. The page says openly that it belongs to Picsart.
How
Fully automated. Software spots trends and plans, Picsart's AI tools make every result and step, a renderer cuts the posts, a person only approves. About 5 to 60 credits (Picsart's unit of payment for AI generation) per post.
Will it work
Main metric: median views per post against our own new-account baseline. Picsart metric: link taps per 1,000 views. Decision after 12 posts over 4 weeks: scale at 3× the baseline, kill under 1.5×.
Evidence
Strong on demand: the top AI image searches of 2025 were all "make X with AI" recreations. Weak on precedent: every large creative-tutorial page has a presenter; no faceless one exists at scale, so we are first.

The six post types, with the real examples we made

A pillar is a recurring type of post. One trend gives a thread: the same trend told six ways across a week. Below: the "Save Yourself" thread (a viral edit where you pull yourself up a cliff), as the pipeline produced it. The hook is the line on screen in the first second. People and dog are AI-generated and labelled; every result is a real Picsart output.

1 · Trend Steal, 25 s. The reach. A trend rebuilt within 72 hours in AI Playground (Picsart's hub of AI models): selfie, scene with the Nano Banana Pro image model, motion with the Kling V3 video model. Caption ends "Comment STEAL and I'll send both prompts. made with Picsart".
2 · 3-Step Fix, 17 s. The search. An everyday job people search for, here "remove sunglasses from a photo": before/after, one edit prompt, then Enhance. Caption ends "Save this for your next trend."
3 · Prompt Drop, 7 slides. The save. A carousel (images swiped sideways): the result, the trick, both prompts in full, two remixes, the Recipe Card.
4 · Model Fight, 23 s. The debate. Same picture and prompt in four video models inside AI Playground, credits shown for each. Caption: "Which one wins? Comment 1, 2, 3 or 4."
5 · Build Once, 21 s. The upgrade. The edit as a Flow (Picsart's canvas where you connect steps once, then rerun them on new photos), run on three people. The canvas here is a drawing of the chain; a real Flow capture replaces it before launch.
6 · You Asked, 23 s. The conversation. Answers the week's top "can I do this with…?" comment: here a dog does the rescue. Posted only as a TikTok reply to a real viewer comment.

Every post ends on the Recipe Card: tool and path, steps, time, credits, how easy the start is, a "works as of" date, the comment keyword.

Who it is for, and the market

49%of US consumers have used TikTok as a search engine, up from 41% in 2024; 65% of Gen Z. People look up "how to make this" where they saw it. Adobe, Feb 2026 (807 people) · Gen Z figure via SEJ, Feb 2026

500M+images and 23 million first-time users in about two weeks after one image model's launch (Nano Banana, Sep 2025). Trend waves are huge and short. TechCrunch, Sep 2025

Why they come back. Trends change weekly, so "how do I make this?" never runs out, and each answer works on the viewer's own photo. In the 2025 waves, tutorials posted 2 to 7 days after a trend began caught the peak (our read of public post dates, estimate). Between waves, the everyday fixes keep the page useful.

Competitors and the gap. The fastest-growing tutorial pages recreate viral effects, but all have a presenter and push other tools: @stevenwommack (about 6.7M Instagram), @withgeorgy (about 901K, roughly double an earlier reported 422K), @blake.ridder (about 864K). The big faceless AI pages, @evolving.ai (about 4.8M) and @chatgptricks (about 2.7M), post AI news, not how-to (all read 2 Oct 2026). Tool brands pay outside creators for tutorials. Picsart's own YouTube result-first AI tutorials reach 55K to 137K views, while its recent Shorts get about 3K to 7K (read 2 Oct 2026): the format works, the short version is missing. Nobody runs a faceless, numbered tutorial page where every step happens in one app.

How it is made

Per post

1 result + 2 to 4 steps + Recipe Card · about 5 to 60 credits (fixes cheapest, model comparisons most) · 2 to 3 human minutes.

Per week

10 posts per platform, same assets: 3 Trend Steal, 2 Fix, 2 Prompt Drop, 1 each of the rest. About 200 to 300 credits (estimate), 20 human minutes plus replies.

Rules on every post

AI label in frame and the platform's AI flag set · caption ends "made with Picsart" · at most five hashtags · Picsart ownership in name and bio · "works as of" date · no reposted clips, no real people's faces, no copyrighted characters.

Will it work, and how we'll know

Hypothesis. Creators will follow, save and share a faceless page that shows a viral edit and the exact Picsart steps, and a meaningful share will tap through and make their own version.

First experiment. Call to action on Prompt Drop: "Comment STEAL for the prompts" against "Save this". Six posts per arm, metric link taps per 1,000 views, guardrail saves, pre-registered. Next, once product allows: drawn step panels against real screen captures of Picsart.

Scale when, after at least 12 posts over 4 weeks, median views exceed 3× the baseline and followers are growing: move toward two posts a day.
Kill when, after 12 posts, median views are under 1.5× the baseline and no post reached 10× the page's own median: archive the kit, keep the account, replace from the backlog within a week.

No tuning before the 12-post read except fixing broken AI disclosures. Honest read: demand is strong and the examples cost little; the view floor is a guess until week two and the comment-to-link reply is not set up yet.
